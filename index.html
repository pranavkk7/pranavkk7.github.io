Main project

Unique Fitness Gym

The front-desk app of a gym in Pinarayi, Kerala, used there every day on the gym owner's Android phone and tablet. It handles admissions, payments, dues and WhatsApp reminders and keeps its data on the device. It also includes a client for the gym's eSSL face-recognition door, tested against a simulated terminal; the door itself isn't connected yet.

  • Flutter and DartOffline-first, data kept on the device with Hive
  • Android, iOS, webOn the gym owner's Android phone and tablet; an iPhone build checked in CI; the web build is the live demo
  • 99 automated testsRun by CI on every push to main
  • eSSL / ZKTeco clientThe door's network protocol, implemented in Dart

The day at a glance

One screen answers the owner's first questions: how many came in, what was collected, how many plans end this week. The check-in log draws a tick for every visit since 5 AM.

The face-recognition door

I wrote my own Dart client for the terminal's network protocol, working from the open-source pyzk project: packet framing, checksums and the password handshake. Tests compare key packets with pyzk's bytes and run the client against a fake terminal over TCP.

Once the door is connected, the app adds members to the terminal, sees when their face is registered, pulls entries into attendance every five minutes and blocks expired plans at each sync. Until then, a simulated terminal stands in for it in the tests and the demo.

An admission in four steps

Details, health notes, a plan showing its saving against paying monthly, then cash, UPI or card and a PDF receipt. A part-payment becomes a due the app remembers.

WhatsApp reminders

The Reminders tab lists expiring plans, dues, birthdays and members who stopped coming, and a morning notification sums up the day. WhatsApp opens with each message written; the desk only taps send.

The owner's numbers

Income against a monthly target, profit after expenses, how members pay and the busy hours, behind an owner PIN.

Cravio

Food delivery where an AI assistant builds your cart from a plain-English request.

  • Ask for "biryani under ₹300 that reaches me in 30 minutes": the assistant searches the demo menus through five server-side tools, suggests matching dishes and adds the ones you pick to your cart. Checkout always stays with the customer.
  • Only restaurants that deliver to your location, nearest first, with delivery times; out-of-range orders are refused on the server.
  • Role-based access for customers, vendors and admins, Razorpay payments verified on the server, and 85 automated tests.

PolySub

Subtitles in about 100 languages, using whichever speech model measured more accurate.

  • I benchmarked models on Google's FLEURS: Qwen3-ASR won for English (3.8% word error rate) and Whisper large-v3 for Japanese (4.1% character error rate).
  • An LLM translates in numbered batches and must return one line per subtitle, so every subtitle keeps its timing. Outputs SRT, WebVTT, bilingual files or video with subtitles burned in.
  • Speech recognition runs locally on my RTX 4060; translation calls an LLM API. A Gradio web page, and 44 tests that run without a GPU.

Python, PyTorch, Qwen3-ASR, Whisper, LLM API, FFmpeg, Gradio
